Proposal 1: Settlement Fault-Line Grid

Visual metaphor: A 6-row × 5-column heat-map grid — categories on the Y axis, settlement assumptions on the X axis — cell darkness encodes entry count.

Data source: category field × coded_dimensions.settlement_assumption (5 values: crypto-native, fiat, hybrid, neutral, unstated). Raw counts from corpus: A=[0,12,1,0,1], B=[0,1,0,0,7], Ccr=[10,0,1,0,0], Cs=[0,0,0,1,0], D=[2,0,1,0,4], E=[0,0,0,3,7].

Why it works: The grid makes the corpus's central empirical claim — that settlement assumption is the primary fault line — immediately legible without reading a word. The near-total contrast between Ccr (crypto column full, fiat column empty) and Category A (fiat column full, crypto column empty) is a visual argument that rewards scrutiny. A heat-map requires no JS, renders cleanly as pure SVG rectangles, and survives 375 px by collapsing to a tall portrait strip.

Proposal 2: Definition Timeline (2018–2026)

Visual metaphor: A horizontal timeline strip — each of the 51 definitions is a vertical tick mark at its date_iso position, coloured by category, height proportional to cross-reference count.

Data source: date_iso (range 2018-06 to 2026-03), category (colour key), cross_references length (tick height). Actual year distribution: 2018=1, 2021=3, 2024=1, 2025=25, 2026=21.

Why it works: The corpus's intellectual history is its most under-communicated feature — 46 of 51 definitions appeared after January 2025, which is itself an argument about the field's immaturity. A timeline encodes this density shock without any prose. Tick-height as influence (cross-reference count) adds a second dimension at zero visual cost. The horizontal axis reads naturally on both desktop and portrait mobile without requiring any interaction.

Proposal 3: Actor × Temporal Scatter

Visual metaphor: A scatter plot — X axis = temporal horizon (present / near-future / speculative), Y axis = actor model (human-in-loop / hybrid / autonomous), each dot = one entry coloured by category, radius = 1 (all dots equal weight to avoid false influence signal).

Data source: coded_dimensions.actor_model (4 values), coded_dimensions.temporal_horizon (3 values), category (colour). Counts: autonomous=31, human-in-loop=13, hybrid=5, ambiguous=2. Temporal: present=22, near-future=24, speculative=5.

Why it works: The actor model × temporal horizon quadrant surfaces a structural tension the corpus text only hints at: Category A definitions (human-in-loop, present) cluster in the bottom-left, while Category Ccr (autonomous, near-future / speculative) clusters top-right — a diagonal that embodies the field's ideological split. The scatter requires no JS, no libraries, no animation; every dot is a fixed SVG circle. At 375 px mobile, the three-column X axis compresses to a readable strip. The quadrant metaphor is academically grounded in Rogers diffusion and Gartner hype-cycle framing.

The Two Economies

A visual breakdown of the 51 definitions organized by their economic structure: the human economy (where agents work for humans) and the machine economy (where agents transact autonomously).

The Human Economy

22 definitions spanning two categories. Category A: Agents buy FOR humans. Category B: Agents replace human workers.

The Machine Economy

12 definitions where agents transact with each other autonomously. 11 assume blockchain; 1 (BotNode) does not.

The 11:1 Ratio

A defining structural fact: blockchain dominates Category C definitions, with only BotNode as the non-crypto alternative.

Infrastructure (D & E)

17 definitions focus on protocols, governance, and infrastructure supporting both economies.

Market Scale

The human economy projects to $3-5 trillion by 2030 (McKinsey), driven by Stripe, Visa, and Mastercard integration.
